**Measured reason for missing the target:** the primary transformer's class scores are risk **0.827**, neutral **0.774**, and opportunity **0.557**. Their arithmetic mean is **0.719**, leaving **0.081** to the proposed 0.80 macro target. Opportunity is the largest class-level weakness: it finds **27/51**, while **19/46** of its opportunity predictions are false alarms against the dataset. Improving risk recall alone therefore does not satisfy the balanced-quality gate.

If risk and neutral F1 stayed exactly fixed, opportunity F1 would need to reach about **0.799** to make the mean 0.80. This is an arithmetic illustration, not a forecast or an independently achievable intervention: changing predictions also changes other classes' precision and recall. The dashed line in the figure is a reference to the **average** target; the rule does not require every individual class to exceed 0.80.

**What we know about possible causes:** the opportunity→neutral examples in Section 8 show that both specific initiatives and future aspirations can be missed. Training contains 200 opportunity paragraphs versus 326 neutral paragraphs, which could limit coverage of opportunity wording, but no learning-curve experiment isolates that effect. The 128-token limit truncates 57/320 test paragraphs, but their transformer accuracy is 45/57, higher than the overall 243/320; it cannot explain all observed shortfall. The recorded validation F1 of seed 42 reaches 0.842, while test F1 is 0.719; that difference is consistent with validation-selection optimism or different paragraph composition, and does not by itself establish overfitting or label noise. Sources: `split_manifest.csv`, `tables/error_slices.csv`, seed-42 `trainer_state.json`, and the full error examples.

**Decision implication:** the prespecified model misses the declared project gate in this experiment. It has not demonstrated the proposed level of balanced reliability for unattended use. This is not proof that all its suggestions are wrong, that 0.80 is a universal safety boundary, or that more training would necessarily fix it. A better-targeted data/model experiment and a fresh holdout are needed before changing the recommendation.
